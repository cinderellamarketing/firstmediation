Every client, booking and payment in one place

Capsule already runs your client journey. Over three months you move into a brand new account, set up for the team, and it takes over the spreadsheets too. The whole team works from one account, and you can see every case at once.

Prepared for Lesa Clarke  ·  First Mediation  ·  October 2026
1 Where things stand today▶

Capsule already runs the business. Every enquiry becomes a case, named after the first client and later the second. Your 35 tracks set out each step from the first call to the final session, and the team works through the task list on the home page each day. Around 70 tasks are completed every weekday, and more than 100 on the busiest days. More than four in five of them come straight from a track. Very little slips: of almost 300 open tasks, fewer than 30 are overdue.

What Capsule doesn't do yet is give you the overview. Mediator bookings and fees, vouchers, legal aid and Global Payments are each kept on a spreadsheet. Seven steps in your tracks tell the team to copy details across to them. Capsule shows one client at a time, so the spreadsheets are the only place you see everyone at once.

The whole team shares one login, and task categories named after people stand in for separate logins. So Capsule can't show who did what, or which mediator is working with which client.

The account also holds years of finished work. Cases have only been closed since January, nothing has ever been deleted, and old tags and fields have built up along the way.

You were clear that the time goes on the spreadsheets, not on winning clients. Most people who call go on to book, but every booking is then typed in twice, and everyone who joins the team has to learn Capsule and the spreadsheets. That is what this proposal sets out to remove.

3 Month one: a new account, set up for your team▶

We build the new account before anything moves, so it is ready and checked when the team arrives in it.

Two logins

  • The new account has two logins: one shared by you and Yvonne, and one shared by Stacey and Danielle. The mediators don't get one.
  • Each login gets an email invitation from Capsule and its own password. The account is in your name, and you are its administrator.
  • Each of the four of you has a task category with your name on it, so every task shows who it is for, even though two people share a login. The task list and the calendar can be filtered to one person's name.
  • The tracks are set so each task lands with the right login and the right person, following the split we agree with you.

One board for every case

  • A case board with a column for each stage, from enquiry through each MIAM and mediation to closed. We agree the stages with you, starting from the lettered status tags you use now.
  • The status tags aren't carried over. Each live case arrives in the column for the stage it has reached. 106 open cases carry more than one status tag at the moment, so we check those with you before the move.
  • Moving a case on is a drag from one column to the next.

The mediator on every case

  • Each case has a Mediator field, with a drop-down list of your mediators. New mediators are added to the list as they join.
  • The mediator is chosen on the case when the MIAM is booked. For the live cases, it is filled in during the move with Stacey's help.
  • This is what the workload and upsell figures in section 6 depend on, which is why it comes first.

Intake details in one place

  • The details taken at enquiry, such as how the client found you and the type of dispute, are asked on both the client and the case today. About a third of cases have none of them filled in.
  • In the new account they live on the case only. Anything filled in on the client in the old account is brought across onto the case, so nothing is lost.

Your tracks

  • All 35 of your tracks are copied into the new account, with their steps and timings as they are now.
  • The seven steps that point to a spreadsheet are rewritten in month two.

What changes for the team

  • Stacey and Danielle log in with their login, and you and Yvonne with yours. Each person filters the task list to their own name to see their tasks, and anyone can still see everyone's.
  • To see where a case has reached, they open the board instead of reading tags.
  • Booking a MIAM includes choosing the mediator on the case.
  • The tracks run as they do now.

What we need from you

  • The email address each of the two logins should use.
  • Time at the kick-off with you, Stacey and Yvonne to agree the board stages, the task categories and how tasks are split between the team.
  • The current list of mediators.
4 Month one: the changeover▶

Mediation is without prejudice, and you tell clients their information doesn't stay with you. The current account holds a lot of finished work: of its 587 open cases, 440 have no task left on them and 318 are more than a year old. So rather than moving everything and then cleaning up, only what the business still needs goes into the new account.

What comes across

  • Every live case, with the people on it, its open tasks, its notes and the details recorded on it.
  • Finished cases still inside the period you keep data for. They arrive closed, so the deletion routine below picks them up when their time comes.
  • Your referrers: the solicitor firms and other organisations that send you work, with their contacts.

Before the move, we confirm with you exactly what comes across, and anything that can't be carried over exactly as it is.

What stays behind

  • Finished cases past the period you keep data for, and the people only on those cases.
  • The lettered status tags, the old fields nobody uses, and the unused sales pipeline with its 10 opportunities.

Step by step

  1. Confirm what is finished. We list the open cases with no task left. You or Stacey confirm which are finished.
  2. Agree the rule. You decide how long after a case ends its data is kept.
  3. Your sign-off. We produce the list of cases and people that will stay behind. Nothing is left behind until you have approved it in writing.
  4. A trial run. We move a handful of cases first and check them with Stacey in the new account.
  5. Changeover day. On a day agreed with you, the team stops working in the old account at an agreed time. We move everything across, and the team starts in the new account the next morning.
  6. QuickBooks. We connect QuickBooks to the new account with Yvonne, and check it before the team starts.

The old account

  • It stays open, untouched, for six months after changeover day, just in case anything needs checking. Nobody works in it.
  • After six months it is deleted in full, with everything that was left behind in it.
  • It is cut down to a single user for those six months, so only one login is paid for until it is deleted.

The deletion routine in the new account

  • The last step on each case track marks the case ready for deletion, once the client has paid and the mediator's invoice is settled.
  • A saved list shows every case past the point you agreed. Deleting becomes a regular task for the team, done from that list, rather than an occasional clear-out.
  • Monthly totals are kept without names, such as the work done by each mediator and the MIAMs that went on to mediation. So the figures in section 6 survive the deletion.
  • Capsule keeps anything deleted in its Trash for 30 days. After that it is gone for good.

QuickBooks keeps its own copy of names and addresses for invoicing. What stays there is for Yvonne and your accountant to decide.

What we need from you

  • Confirmation of which open cases are finished.
  • The rule for how long data is kept, and your written sign-off on what stays behind.
  • A changeover day that suits the team, and Stacey's time to check the trial run.
  • Yvonne, or whoever looks after QuickBooks, to connect it to the new account.

Your tracks already follow the spreadsheets closely. Seven steps across four tracks tell the team to add a booking, log a payment or record legal aid progress on a spreadsheet: two in Pre C1 MIAM, one in Pre C2 MIAM, one in Pre Mediation and three in UC Legal Aid. Each one is rewritten to update the case instead, so the team keeps working through the tracks they know.

One spreadsheet at a time

  1. We go through the spreadsheet's column headings with Stacey and Yvonne, and agree what each becomes in Capsule. We only need the headings, not the client details in the rows.
  2. We add those details to the case, and build the list that replaces the spreadsheet.
  3. We rewrite the matching track steps.
  4. The team is shown how to use it.
  5. The team uses Capsule instead. The spreadsheet is retired once they're happy with what replaces it.

7 Month three: referrers, enquiries and handover▶

Referrers in the new account

  • The contacts in the separate marketing account come across into the new account, so there is one place to look and one subscription to pay.
  • Each solicitor firm is recorded as an organisation with its solicitors under it. Each is tagged tier 1, 2 or 3, as you grade them now, or as a prospect if they have never referred.
  • Citizens Advice, psychologists and other referrers get a tag for their type. Filtering by a tag shows any group in one list.
  • Solicitor prospecting runs as cases in the current account, so it sits in the same list as your clients. In the new account it runs from the referrers' own contact records instead.
  • Marketing follow-ups get their own task category. The team can filter them out of the client task list, which was the reason the second account was started.

Newsletters by group

  • Transpond, Capsule's email add-on, sends to the same contacts, using the tags. So solicitors can get one newsletter and Citizens Advice something different.
  • We set up the first newsletter template with you.
  • Transpond can also schedule your LinkedIn posts.

The MIAM request form on your website

  • A MIAM request form on your website creates the enquiry in Capsule, ready for the team to call back and book, so nobody types it in again.
  • How it connects depends on what your website is built on. We find that out, tell your web designers exactly what to use, and connect it to Capsule once they have built it.
  • Training enquiries can follow the same route when you are ready.

Handover

  • A final review of everything with the team.
  • Your guide page holds every session recording and how each part works. Stacey, Danielle and Yvonne, and anyone who joins later, learn one system rather than Capsule plus the spreadsheets.
  • Once the marketing contacts have moved across, the marketing account and the older free account can be closed. The main old account follows six months after changeover day.

What we need from you

  • Working login details for the marketing account, as the current ones need resetting.
  • A contact at your web designers.
  • Your tier for each solicitor firm, where it isn't already recorded.

The tracks. They cover the whole client journey: the enquiry, each client's MIAM, the mediation itself, vouchers, legal aid and court forms. Over the last 90 days the team completed almost 5,000 tasks, and more than four in five came straight from a track.

The task list is under control. Of 297 open tasks, 29 are overdue and none by more than a month. At this volume that is a well-run list.

Cases are now being closed as well. That started in January, and around 60 a month have been closed since.

2 The points that shaped this proposal▶

The spreadsheets are the priority. You don't need a sales pipeline. Enquiries come through the Family Mediation Council, the government website and local solicitors, and you convert them yourself. What costs time is copying Capsule's information into spreadsheets in order to see everyone at once.

One account. The second account started because marketing follow-ups were swamping client work. Tags and filtered task views solve that inside one account, and the team gets one place to look.

A login each. The admin team works in Capsule every day and Yvonne looks after the finance side, so separate logins replace the colour coding by shift.

The question you most want answered is which mediators turn a MIAM into further sessions. That needs the mediator recorded on every case, which is why it comes first.

Deleting matters as much as recording. Mediation is without prejudice, so client data has to go when a case ends, and the setup has to make that routine.

The business is growing. More mediators are joining, taking you to nine, a training company has started, and you want to step back from running appointments yourself. The system has to work without you in it every day.
