Every client, booking and payment in one place

Capsule already runs your client journey. Over three months it takes over the spreadsheets too, so the whole team works from one account and you can see every case at once.

Prepared for Lesa Clarke  ·  First Mediation
1 Where things stand today▶

Capsule already runs the business. Every enquiry becomes a case, your 35 tracks set out each step from first call to final session, and the team completes around 70 tasks every weekday, more than 100 on the busiest days. Very little slips: of almost 300 open tasks, fewer than 30 are overdue.

What Capsule doesn't do yet is give you the overview. Bookings, vouchers, legal aid and Global Payments are kept on spreadsheets, and several of your tracks tell the team to copy details across to them. Capsule shows one client at a time, and the spreadsheets are the only place you see everyone at once.

The whole team shares one login, and task categories stand in for people. So Capsule can't show who did what, or which mediator is working with which client.

You were clear that the time goes on the spreadsheets, not on winning clients. That is what this proposal sets out to remove.

3 Month one: a clean account, set up for your team▶

A login each. Everyone who works in Capsule gets their own login. Tasks are assigned to a person rather than a coloured category, and Capsule records who completed each one. Task categories go back to describing the kind of task.

One board for every case. Cases move across a board in stages, from enquiry through each MIAM and mediation to closed, so every live case is on one screen. The board replaces the lettered status tags, which have drifted over time: some cases now carry more than one.

The mediator on every case. At the moment a mediator only appears as a task category. A mediator field on each case is what the workload and upsell figures in month two depend on.

The details taken at enquiry, such as how the client found you and the type of dispute, are asked in two places at the moment, on the client and on the case. They move to one place, the case, so they are filled in once and can be reported on.

4 Month one: closing finished cases and clearing old records▶

Mediation is without prejudice, and you tell clients their information doesn't stay with you. Cases have only been closed in Capsule since January, and nothing has been deleted, so the account holds a lot of finished work. Most open cases have no task left on them, which usually means the work is done but the case was never closed.

The clean-up runs in this order, and nothing is deleted until you have approved the list:

  • Go through the open cases with no task left, and close the finished ones
  • Agree the rule with you: how long after a case ends its data goes
  • Produce the list of every case and client past that point, for you to check and sign off
  • Delete what you have signed off: the case and the people on it, since case and track names sometimes carry client details too

Then the routine keeps it that way. The last step on each case track marks the case ready for deletion once the client has paid and the mediator's invoice is settled. A saved list shows everything past that point, so deleting becomes a regular task rather than an occasional clear-out. Monthly totals are kept without names, so the mediator and upsell figures survive the deletion.

QuickBooks keeps its own copy of names and addresses for invoicing. What stays there is for Yvonne and your accountant to decide.

5 Month two: the spreadsheets come into Capsule▶

Your tracks already follow the spreadsheets closely. Seven track steps tell the team to add a booking, log a payment or record legal aid progress on a spreadsheet. Each one is rewritten to update the case instead, so the team keeps working through the tracks they know.

6 Seeing the whole business▶

Once the mediator and the bookings are on the case, the questions you asked on the call can be answered from Capsule:

  • Which mediator is doing the most work, month by month
  • Which clients go on from the MIAM to further sessions, mediator by mediator, so you can see who is upselling
  • Money due in from clients and voucher claims, and what is owed to each mediator
  • Where every live case has reached, from the board

Some of these come from Capsule's reports, and others from saved lists you can open at any time. Anything that needs a calculation across them, such as the margin on each mediator's work, comes from an export.

7 Month three: referrers, enquiries and handover▶

Referrers tagged by type. Prospecting already runs in Capsule, through the solicitor tracks, but as cases, so it sits in the same list as your clients. Instead, referrers are followed from their own contact records. Each firm is recorded as an organisation with its solicitors under it, tagged as tier 1, 2 or 3 or as a prospect. Citizens Advice, psychologists and other referrers get their own tags, so filtering by a tag shows any group in one list. The contacts in the separate marketing account come across too.

Newsletters by group. Transpond, Capsule's email add-on, sends to the same contacts: one newsletter for solicitors, something different for Citizens Advice, and so on. We set up the first template with you. Transpond can also schedule your LinkedIn posts.

A MIAM request form on your website creates the enquiry in Capsule, ready for the team to call back and book, so nobody types it in again. How it connects depends on what your website is built on, so we will tell your web designers exactly what to use. Training enquiries can follow the same route when you are ready.

Every session is recorded and written up on your own guide page. Stacey, Danielle and Yvonne, and anyone who joins later, learn one system rather than Capsule plus the spreadsheets.

1 What is working▶

The tracks. They cover the whole client journey: the enquiry, each client's MIAM, the mediation itself, vouchers, legal aid and court forms. Over the last 90 days the team completed almost 5,000 tasks, and more than four in five came straight from a track.

The task list is under control. Of 297 open tasks, 29 are overdue and none by more than a month. At this volume that is a well-run list.

Cases are now being closed as well. That started in January, and around 60 a month have been closed since.

2 What gets in the way▶
  • One login. Every task, open or done, is recorded against the same user, so Capsule can't show who did what. Task categories named after people stand in for logins instead.
  • No mediator on the case. There is no field for it. A mediator only appears as a task category.
  • The spreadsheets are written into the tracks. Seven track steps tell the team to copy details to a spreadsheet.
  • No overview of cases. There is no board. Status is kept in lettered tags. Only two are used much, and some cases carry more than one.
  • Intake details in two places. How the client found you is asked on both the client and the case, and around a third of cases have none of these details filled in.
  • Prospecting mixed in with clients. Solicitor prospecting runs as cases, so it shares a list with client work. The tier 1, 2 and 3 split isn't recorded in Capsule.
  • An unused sales pipeline. It holds 10 opportunities, none updated since 2025. You told us you don't need one.

2 The points that shaped this proposal▶

The spreadsheets are the priority. You don't need a sales pipeline. Enquiries come through the Family Mediation Council, the government website and local solicitors, and you convert them yourself. What costs time is copying Capsule's information into spreadsheets in order to see everyone at once.

One account. The second account started because marketing follow-ups were swamping client work. Tags and filtered task views solve that inside one account, and the team gets one place to look.

A login each. The admin team works in Capsule every day and Yvonne looks after the finance side, so separate logins replace the colour coding by shift.

The question you most want answered is which mediators turn a MIAM into further sessions. That needs the mediator recorded on every case, which is why it comes first.

Deleting matters as much as recording. Mediation is without prejudice, so client data has to go when a case ends, and the setup has to make that routine.

The business is growing. More mediators are joining, taking you to nine, a training company has started, and you want to step back from running appointments yourself. The system has to work without you in it every day.
